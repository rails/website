Happy to report that in _record time_, all **Rails World 2026** talks are now online. 

<a href="https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLdMRFKO1zSBE">Find the full playlist here</a>.

While all four keynotes got everyone talking about AI, this year’s talks covered a good balance of what's new in Rails _and_ how Rails developers are using AI.

The AI-focused talks covered: making codebases agent-friendly, harness engineering, generators, AI pipelines in plain Ruby, agent loops driving Ractor-safety, building MCPs, and rapid deploys with the help of agents. 

There were also talks about: **Lexxy**, **Active Search**, **ONCE**, **Herb** in Rails 8.2 and durable **Active Job workflows**, plus deep dives into **Solid Cable**, **Hotwire**, **Kamal**, sharding, the **RubyGems compact index**, and **HotCell** (which could technically fall into both categories since it was built in response to an increase in AI-assisted threats).

Find them all on the playlist above. Enjoy watching!

# Recap

More than **1,000 Rails devs** from **58 countries** came to Austin for the very first Rails World in the US - and our largest yet. For more than two days we had technical talks, networking, the Rails at Scale Summit, and a fantastic closing party at Banger’s. 

<img src="/assets/images/RW26-collage-1.jpg">

There was a **mechanical bull**, **live music** during the lunch breaks, **BBQ & taco food trucks**, **a vinyl record** made specifically for the first US Rails World, lots of **neon** (my personal fave), a fun old Western twist on the Ruby Embassy (**a Sheriff’s office**), and a closing performance by **beatboxer Yuri Lane**.

One last big thank you to all of our [speakers](/world/2026/speakers) and sponsors (<a href="https://rubyonrails.org/2026/9/18/rails-world-2026-livestream-and-sponsors">listed here</a>). You’ve made this Rails World one for the books!
